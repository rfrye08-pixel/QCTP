# QCTP Breathwork and Respiratory Control System Rev0

**Document ID:** QCTP-BREATH-REV0

**Status:** CONTROLLED PRODUCT AND CURRICULUM REQUIREMENT  
**Applies to:** QCTP Platform Rev2, Foundation Path, Practice Player, Focus 10/OBE, Remote Viewing, Psionics, Studio, Mirror, and daily integration  
**Release impact:** No change to the released Rev1.1.4 Day 1 timeline until implemented on the isolated production branch and regression-tested.

## 1. User need

Ryan does not yet have a reliable decision system for choosing a breathing method. QCTP currently uses phrases such as “breathe slowly,” “enter coherence,” or “settle the body” without fully teaching:

- how to inhale and exhale mechanically;
- how much air to move;
- what cadence to use;
- whether to use the nose or mouth;
- when an exhale-biased pattern is appropriate;
- when equal breathing is better;
- when to use a physiological sigh;
- when to use or avoid breath retention;
- how breathing changes for meditation, Focus 10, OBE, remote viewing, sleep, emotional regulation, energy work, and daily life;
- how to recognize overbreathing, air hunger, or excessive effort;
- how to calibrate a personal baseline.

Breath control is therefore a core QCTP skill system, not a minor meditation instruction.

## 2. Product principle

Breathing is the fastest voluntary actuator QCTP has for changing state.

The operating loop is:

`ASSESS STATE -> SELECT BREATH MODE -> EXECUTE WITH LOW EFFORT -> RELEASE CONTROL -> OBSERVE EFFECT -> LOG -> ADAPT`

QCTP shall never present one breathing method as correct for every objective.

## 3. Immediate controlled default

Until Ryan completes personal calibration, QCTP shall use the following default unless a module explicitly requires another pattern.

### QCTP-B1 — Foundation Resonance Breath

**Primary use:** morning settling, coherence, pre-meditation, emotional regulation, transition into open awareness.  
**Cadence:** inhale 4 seconds, exhale 6 seconds.  
**Rate:** 6 breaths per minute.  
**Hold:** none.  
**Inhale route:** nose.  
**Exhale route:** nose by default; softly through the mouth if nasal exhalation feels restrictive or the method specifically calls for mouth exhalation.  
**Volume:** comfortable, quiet, approximately 60–75% of a maximal breath—not a full lung inflation.  
**Duration:** 3–5 minutes before meditation; up to 10 minutes when deliberately training the breath itself.

### Exact execution

1. Sit upright with the spine neutral, shoulders loose, jaw unclenched, and tongue relaxed.
2. Place one hand over the lower ribs or upper abdomen during early training.
3. Inhale quietly through the nose for four seconds.
4. Allow the lower ribs, back ribs, and abdomen to expand together. Do not deliberately push the belly outward and do not raise the shoulders.
5. Exhale smoothly for six seconds. Do not squeeze the lungs empty or force the last air out.
6. Allow the next inhale to begin without a deliberate retention.
7. Continue for three to five minutes.
8. After the final paced exhale, stop counting and allow the breath to regulate itself.

### Adjustment rule

If the pattern creates air hunger, tension, repeated yawning, dizziness, tingling, or the need to take large recovery breaths:

- reduce breath volume first;
- then change to 5 seconds in / 5 seconds out;
- if still uncomfortable, return to natural breathing.

The objective is slow and quiet breathing, not large breathing.

## 4. The QCTP breath-selection matrix

### 4.1 General morning regulation

**Method:** QCTP-B1, 4 in / 6 out, no hold.  
**Duration:** 5 minutes.  
**Transition:** release counting and move into open focus or the Gap.

### 4.2 Acute stress, irritation, panic-like activation, or before a difficult conversation

**Method:** one to three physiological sighs, followed by QCTP-B1 for one to three minutes.

A physiological sigh is:

1. inhale through the nose to a comfortable near-full level;
2. take one smaller second nasal inhale to top off;
3. exhale slowly and completely through the mouth;
4. return to ordinary breathing or begin 4/6 breathing.

Do not perform repeated maximal gasping. The second inhale is a small top-off, not another full breath.

### 4.3 Five-minute emotional downshift session

**Method:** QCTP-B2 Cyclic Sighing.  
**Pattern:** gentle double nasal inhale, long mouth exhale.  
**Duration:** up to 5 minutes.  
**Use:** when the primary goal is reducing activation rather than entering a silent meditative state.

### 4.4 Balanced focus before CAD, geometry, source study, remote viewing, or analytical work

**Method:** QCTP-B3 Balanced Breath.  
**Cadence:** 5 seconds inhale / 5 seconds exhale.  
**Hold:** none.  
**Duration:** 2–4 minutes.  
**Transition:** release pacing and begin the task.

This is less sedating than a strongly extended exhale and does not introduce breath holds that can become distracting.

### 4.5 Meditation and Gap practice

**Method:** QCTP-B1 for 3–5 minutes.  
**Critical transition:** once the body is settled, stop controlling the breath. The Gap is not practiced by continuing to count indefinitely.

The sequence is:

`4/6 PACING -> QUIET NATURAL BREATH -> OPEN FOCUS -> GAP`

### 4.6 Focus 10 and OBE preparation

**Method:** QCTP-B1 for 3–5 minutes while seated or lying safely.  
**Then:** release all deliberate breath control and allow breathing to become automatic while awareness remains present.

Counting for the entire session can keep the analytical mind active. The breath is used to enter the state, then released.

No forceful hyperventilation or prolonged retention belongs in the Foundation Focus 10/OBE sequence.

### 4.7 Sleep preparation

**Method:** 4 seconds inhale / 6 seconds exhale.  
**Duration:** 5–10 minutes.  
**Route:** nasal breathing if comfortable.  
**Effort:** extremely low.  
**Then:** stop counting and sleep.

If 4/6 creates air hunger while lying down, use 5/5 or natural breathing.

### 4.8 Drowsiness when alertness is required

**Method:** QCTP-B3 Balanced Breath, 4/4 or 5/5, no holds.  
**Duration:** 1–3 minutes.  
**Posture:** upright, eyes open, broader visual field.

QCTP shall not use fast forceful breathing as the default alertness tool.

### 4.9 Box breathing for composure and deliberate control

**Method:** QCTP-B4 Box Breath.  
**Cadence:** inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold empty 4.  
**Duration:** 2–4 minutes.  
**Prerequisite:** comfortable with QCTP-B1 and QCTP-B3 without air hunger.  
**Use:** composure, deliberate attention, and practicing controlled transitions.

Box breathing is optional, not the universal default. If the holds create tension, reduce them to 2 seconds or return to no-hold breathing.

### 4.10 Alternate nostril breathing

**Method:** QCTP-B5 Alternate Nostril Breath.  
**Foundation version:** no breath retention.

One cycle:

1. close the right nostril and inhale left for 4 seconds;
2. switch and exhale right for 4 seconds;
3. inhale right for 4 seconds;
4. switch and exhale left for 4 seconds.

Repeat five cycles initially.

**Use:** attention training, ritualized state transition, left/right sensory focus, and later Grant/geometry or contemplative sessions.

### 4.11 Walking integration

**Method:** QCTP-B6 Step Breath.  
**Cadence:** inhale for 3 comfortable steps, exhale for 4 steps.  
**Use:** calm walking, transition after work, future-self walking, and living-in-the-field practice.

Do not force the cadence uphill or during strenuous work. The breath must remain compatible with the physical demand.

### 4.12 Shop-floor micro-reset

Only while safely stationary, away from cutting, machinery, welding, ladders, vehicles, and hazardous operations:

- perform one physiological sigh; or
- perform three cycles of 4 in / 6 out;
- widen peripheral vision;
- resume normal task breathing.

## 5. Breathing mechanics QCTP must teach

### 5.1 Quiet rather than maximal

Slow breathing does not mean taking the largest possible breath each cycle. Large repeated breaths can increase minute ventilation and create overbreathing even when the rate is slow.

The target sensation is:

- quiet airflow;
- low effort;
- no shoulder lift;
- no strain in the throat;
- no need for repeated recovery breaths;
- a smooth exhale;
- comfortable completion of the session.

### 5.2 Three-dimensional expansion

QCTP shall teach lower-rib and abdominal expansion as a three-dimensional volume:

- front abdomen expands slightly;
- side ribs widen;
- back ribs expand subtly;
- upper chest moves only as needed;
- shoulders remain quiet.

“Belly breathing” shall not be taught as forcefully pushing the abdomen outward.

### 5.3 Exhale quality

The exhale should be:

- smooth;
- longer when down-regulation is desired;
- not squeezed empty;
- not interrupted by throat tension;
- quiet enough that another person nearby would barely hear nasal exhalation.

### 5.4 Natural micro-pauses

A natural pause may occur at the end of an exhale. QCTP shall not force a hold unless the selected method explicitly contains one.

### 5.5 Nasal and mouth routes

**Nasal inhale** is the Foundation default.

**Nasal exhale** is the default for resonance, balanced, sleep, Focus 10, and alternate-nostril work.

**Mouth exhale** is used for physiological sighing and may be used when the user needs a clearly longer releasing exhale.

The app shall state the route explicitly for every practice.

## 6. QCTP personal breath calibration

A single cadence shall not be assumed to be optimal forever. QCTP shall include a Breath Calibration session once Ryan can follow a paced pattern comfortably.

### Calibration conditions

- seated safely;
- no machinery, driving, water, ladder, or physical task;
- no large meal immediately beforehand;
- stop if symptoms appear;
- use the same posture for all trials.

### Calibration sequence

1. **Natural baseline — 2 minutes**  
   Observe without changing the breath.

2. **Balanced trial — 3 minutes**  
   5 seconds in / 5 seconds out.

3. **Exhale-biased trial — 3 minutes**  
   4 seconds in / 6 seconds out.

4. **Slower exhale-biased trial — 3 minutes**  
   5 seconds in / 7 seconds out, only if comfortable.

5. **Recovery — 2 minutes**  
   Natural breathing.

### Rate each trial from 0–5

- physical ease;
- calm;
- mental clarity;
- air hunger;
- chest/throat tension;
- sleepiness;
- emotional shift;
- desire to continue.

### Selection rule

The personal default is the pattern with the best combined calm and clarity while:

- air hunger is 0 or 1;
- tension is 0 or 1;
- no dizziness occurs;
- no large recovery breath is required.

If no paced pattern passes, natural breathing remains the baseline and cadence training progresses more gradually.

## 7. QCTP Breath Foundations track

The 112-day Foundation Path remains preserved. Breath Foundations is a required onboarding and parallel skill track rather than a silent rewrite of Day 1.

### BREATH-01 — Mechanics

- posture;
- nasal inhale;
- 360-degree lower-rib expansion;
- quiet breath volume;
- shoulder and throat relaxation;
- five minutes of 5/5 breathing.

### BREATH-02 — Exhale Bias

- compare 5/5 with 4/6;
- notice calm, clarity, air hunger, and effort;
- learn not to force the exhale.

### BREATH-03 — Physiological Sigh

- learn one controlled double inhale and long exhale;
- practice one to three cycles;
- follow with natural breathing;
- use as a rapid state reset.

### BREATH-04 — Cyclic Sighing

- five-minute guided session;
- gentle rather than maximal breaths;
- post-session state log.

### BREATH-05 — Holds and Box Breathing

- understand filled and empty holds;
- begin with 4/2/4/2 if needed;
- progress to 4/4/4/4 only without strain;
- learn when holds are not appropriate.

### BREATH-06 — Alternate Nostril and Walking Breath

- no-hold alternate nostril pattern;
- step-count breathing;
- eyes-open integration.

### BREATH-07 — Personal Calibration and Baseline Lock

- complete the calibration sequence;
- select personal calm cadence;
- select personal focus cadence;
- select acute-reset method;
- store the results in QCTP settings.

## 9. Integration by QCTP domain

### Foundation meditation

Use paced breathing only for the settling/coherence phase, then release control.

### Robert Edward Grant Studio

Use 5/5 balanced breathing for two to three minutes before geometry or number work. This supports alert observation without making the studio session overly sedating.

### Mirror Journal

If emotionally activated, perform one physiological sigh and three 4/6 cycles before recording the raw event. Do not use breathing to erase the emotion; use it to observe the emotion with more control.

### Remote Viewing

Use 5/5 for two to three minutes, then natural breathing. Do not continue counting while recording first impressions.

### Psionics

Use 4/6 for calm sensitivity practice or 5/5 for construct/control work. Advanced forceful breathing is a separate gate.

### Dreams and OBE recall

Do not delay recall with a long breath session. Record first. Regulate afterward if needed.

### Focus 10 / OBE induction

Use 4/6 only during initial body settling. Release control before separation or imagery exercises.

### Walking embodiment

Use 3 steps in / 4 steps out when physically comfortable, then allow natural breathing whenever exertion changes.

## 10. Advanced breathwork hold

The following methods are not part of the QCTP Foundation baseline:

- repeated forceful hyperventilation;
- long maximal inhale holds;
- long empty-lung holds;
- competitive breath-hold training;
- forceful rapid pranayama;
- advanced bandha/lock combinations;
- breathwork performed standing where fainting creates a fall hazard;
- breathwork performed in water;
- breathwork performed while driving or operating machinery.

These may be researched and designed later as an Advanced Breathwork track only after:

- baseline breathing skill is verified;
- contraindication screening is explicit;
- the user understands stop conditions;
- the practice is seated or lying safely;
- the session has its own controlled procedure and release status.

## 11. Stop and recovery conditions

Stop deliberate pacing and return to natural breathing if any of the following appears:

- dizziness;
- visual dimming;
- tingling around the mouth or hands;
- marked air hunger;
- chest pain;
- irregular or alarming heart sensation;
- panic escalation;
- loss of balance;
- confusion;
- inability to maintain the pattern without gasping.

Recovery procedure:

1. stop counting;
2. breathe naturally and quietly;
3. sit or lie safely;
4. do not immediately restart;
5. record the method, cadence, duration, and symptom;
6. reduce volume or choose a less demanding cadence next time.

## 12. Evidence and design basis

Evidence classes remain distinct.

### PUBLISHED — cyclic sighing

Balban et al., *Cell Reports Medicine* (2023), randomized one-month comparison of five-minute daily cyclic sighing, box breathing, cyclic hyperventilation with retention, and mindfulness. Cyclic sighing produced the strongest mood improvement and respiratory-rate reduction among the tested conditions.

- PubMed: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36630953/`
- DOI: `10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895`

### PUBLISHED — slow breathing range

A randomized cross-sectional experiment in 100 healthy adults found the strongest heart-rate-variability measures across approximately five to seven breaths per minute, with no single cadence maximizing every metric.

- PubMed: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34024811/`

### PUBLISHED — inhale/exhale ratio

An experimental study comparing rates and inhale/exhale ratios found that a longer-exhale pattern was associated with greater self-reported relaxation and stress reduction than the opposite ratio.

- PubMed: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25156003/`

### PUBLISHED — breath volume matters

A controlled 2026 experiment separated respiratory rate from minute ventilation and found that breath volume/minute ventilation materially changed cardiovascular responses even when breathing rate was controlled. This supports the QCTP requirement to use slow, quiet, non-maximal breaths rather than repeatedly taking very deep breaths.

- PubMed: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42457770/`

### PUBLISHED — breath-hold caution

A controlled breath-hold study found progressive blood-pressure elevation during deep inspiratory breath holding, supporting the decision to keep prolonged retentions out of the Foundation baseline.

- PubMed: `https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25189121/`

## 13. Acceptance criteria

The QCTP Breathwork system is not complete until:

1. the user can select a goal and receive an exact breath protocol;
2. QCTP-B1, B2, B3, B4, B5, and B6 are represented as structured local data;
3. Breath Foundations BREATH-01 through BREATH-07 exist;
4. every method states cadence, route, volume, duration, posture, transition, and stop conditions;
5. the app distinguishes slow breathing from deep/maximal breathing;
6. the Breath Director blocks practice during hazardous activity;
7. audio, visual, and haptic pacing can work without paid APIs;
8. post-session calibration data persists and exports;
9. personal calm, focus, sleep, and acute-reset profiles can be stored;
10. Foundation Day 1 remains regression-protected;
11. Focus 10/OBE breath guidance releases deliberate control after induction;
12. automated tests cover selection logic, unsafe-state blocking, cadence math, persistence, and export;
13. iPhone portrait and low-screen-interaction use pass device testing.
