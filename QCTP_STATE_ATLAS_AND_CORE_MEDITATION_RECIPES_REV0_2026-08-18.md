## 1. Purpose

QCTP needs a shared state map so that “coherence,” “Gap,” “point consciousness,” “Focus 10,” “Focus 12,” “receiver state,” and “OBE threshold” are not used as interchangeable labels.

This atlas defines:

- the purpose of each state;
- prerequisite states;
- an original QCTP induction recipe;
- state markers;
- common look-alikes;
- correction rules;
- progression gates;
- relationships to source traditions.

These recipes are not reproductions of proprietary Monroe, My Big TOE, HeartMath, Dispenza, or other paid-course scripts. They are original QCTP practices based on publicly described state targets, general attentional training, respiratory-control principles, and QCTP’s controlled requirements.

# 4. Q1 — Regulated Body and Breath

**Type:** QCTP original foundation state  
**Purpose:** reduce unnecessary muscular and respiratory effort while preserving alertness  
**Prerequisite:** safe seated or lying environment  
**Typical duration:** 7 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Minute 0:00–1:00 — Position

- Sit upright with feet supported, or lie down only if sleep is not the immediate risk.
- Let the spine be neutral rather than held rigidly.
- Separate the teeth slightly.
- Let the tongue rest without pressing it against the palate.
- Place one hand over the lower ribs for the first few sessions.

### Minute 1:00–4:00 — Foundation Resonance Breath

- Inhale quietly through the nose for four seconds.
- Use approximately two-thirds of a maximal comfortable breath.
- Allow the lower ribs to widen front, side, and back.
- Keep the shoulders mostly still.
- Exhale smoothly for six seconds through the nose unless nasal exhalation feels restrictive.
- Do not squeeze the lungs empty.
- Do not add a hold.

### Minute 4:00–6:00 — Release control

- Stop counting.
- Let breathing regulate itself.
- Scan jaw, throat, shoulders, hands, abdomen, pelvic floor, thighs, calves, and feet.
- At each location, reduce effort by approximately ten percent on an exhale.
- Do not demand total relaxation.

### Minute 6:00–7:00 — Observe

- Notice whether the breath is quieter.
- Notice whether the face and hands are less effortful.
- Notice whether alertness remains intact.

## State markers

- quiet breathing without recovery gasps;
- lower shoulder and jaw effort;
- stable posture;
- alertness score 2 or higher;
- no dizziness or tingling;
- reduced urgency to move.

## Look-alikes

- overbreathing with large tidal volume;
- slumped drowsiness;
- muscular collapse that creates discomfort;
- breath suppression.

## Corrections

- Air hunger: reduce breath volume first, then use 5 seconds in / 5 seconds out.
- Dizziness or tingling: stop pacing and breathe naturally.
- Sleepiness: sit more upright and use 5/5 rather than 4/6.
- Restlessness: keep eyes softly open for one minute.

## Progression gate

Accessed when quiet pacing is completed for three minutes with low effort and no symptoms. Stabilized when the user can reach a visibly calmer body within five minutes on three of five attempts.

# 5. Q2 — Coherent Affective State

**Type:** QCTP synthesis informed by heart-focused coherence methods  
**Purpose:** deliberately generate and sustain an organized positive emotional state without needing the original story  
**Prerequisite:** Q1 regulated state  
**Typical duration:** 10 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Minute 0:00–3:00 — Regulate

Use Q1 4/6 breathing at quiet volume.

### Minute 3:00–4:00 — Heart-area attention

- Place attention at the center of the chest.
- Do not search for a special sensation.
- Imagine the breath moving through the chest area while the lungs continue working normally.

### Minute 4:00–6:00 — Generate

- Recall one specific memory that reliably produces appreciation, care, gratitude, affection, or quiet awe.
- Use one scene, not a life review.
- Notice the body response: warmth, softening, deeper breath, facial change, emotion behind the eyes, or another clear shift.

### Minute 6:00–8:00 — Remove the story

- Stop replaying the scene.
- Keep attention on the body-state produced by it.
- If the state fades, use the memory for one or two breaths and release it again.

### Minute 8:00–10:00 — Stabilize without paced breathing

- Stop counting.
- Allow natural breathing.
- Sustain the state without verbal affirmations.

## State markers

- emotional/body shift is distinguishable from baseline;
- the state persists for at least 30 seconds after the memory is released;
- breathing becomes naturally smoother or quieter;
- thought speed decreases without dullness;
- the user can name the body signature.

## Look-alikes

- repeating gratitude words without emotional change;
- emotionally intense nostalgia that creates agitation;
- forcing a smile;
- prolonged story replay.

## Corrections

- No emotion: choose a smaller, more concrete memory.
- Excess emotion: shift from love or grief to simple appreciation.
- Story takes over: return to one body sensation in the chest or face.
- State collapses quickly: practice three short memory-to-state transfers instead of one long hold.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when the user can generate a clear state within three minutes and retain it without the story for two continuous minutes in three of five sessions.

# 6. Q3 — Focused Attention

**Type:** standard attentional training  
**Purpose:** hold attention on one selected object, recognize capture, and return efficiently  
**Prerequisite:** Q1  
**Typical duration:** 12 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Minute 0:00–2:00 — Settle

Use natural breathing or three cycles of 4/6. Select one breath location: nostrils, chest, or abdomen. Do not switch locations during the session.

### Minute 2:00–8:00 — Count

- Feel one complete inhale and exhale.
- Count that cycle as one.
- Continue to ten, then return to one.
- When you discover that attention has moved into a thought, sound, or sensation, label once: “captured.”
- Return to the physical breath sensation.
- Restart at one only if the count was lost.

### Minute 8:00–11:00 — Remove counting

- Stop counting.
- Follow the beginning, middle, and end of each breath sensation.
- If captured, return without commentary.

### Minute 11:00–12:00 — Measure

- Estimate average recovery time after recognition.
- Note whether distraction was noticed during the thought or only after a long chain.

## State markers

- breath sensation remains the primary object;
- distraction is recognized;
- return occurs without frustration;
- recovery time trends shorter;
- the user remembers the session continuously.

## Look-alikes

- rigid concentration with forehead tension;
- breath control instead of breath observation;
- counting automatically while thinking about something else;
- suppressing sound or thought.

## Corrections

- Forehead tension: widen the gaze behind closed eyes and reduce effort.
- Breath becomes controlled: take one natural sigh, then observe without changing.
- Constant capture: shorten to five minutes and use tactile breath sensation at the abdomen.
- Drowsiness: eyes slightly open, upright posture, 5/5 breath for one minute.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when the user completes ten minutes, recognizes capture repeatedly, and returns within an estimated ten seconds after recognition in three of five sessions. Zero thoughts are not required.

# 7. Q4 — Open Monitoring and Spatial Awareness

**Type:** QCTP open-focus synthesis aligned with open-monitoring principles  
**Purpose:** include sensory and mental events without selecting or following one  
**Prerequisite:** Q3 accessed  
**Typical duration:** 15 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Minute 0:00–3:00 — Focused anchor

Follow breath sensation at one location. This prevents open monitoring from beginning as diffuse mind-wandering.

### Minute 3:00–5:00 — Head volume

- Notice the three-dimensional volume occupied by the head.
- Include space behind the eyes, between the ears, and above the mouth.
- Do not visualize anatomy.

### Minute 5:00–7:00 — Whole-body volume

- Include chest, abdomen, pelvis, arms, legs, hands, and feet simultaneously.
- Stop scanning in sequence.

### Minute 7:00–10:00 — Room volume

- Include the space around the body and the boundaries of the room.
- Avoid naming objects.
- Let sounds occur at their locations.

### Minute 10:00–14:00 — All events

- Include breath, body sensations, sounds, visual darkness, emotion, and thoughts in one field.
- A thought is treated as an event, not an instruction to continue the story.
- Do not return to one object unless absorption occurs.

### Minute 14:00–15:00 — Close

Notice whether awareness felt broader than ordinary object-focused attention.

## State markers

- more than one sensory channel is held simultaneously;
- sounds and thoughts are noticed without immediate pursuit;
- body and room are experienced as volumes;
- naming decreases;
- awareness remains clear rather than foggy.

## Look-alikes

- zoning out;
- dreamlike drift;
- scanning rapidly between objects;
- trying to imagine the room instead of sensing it.

## Corrections

- Mind-wandering: return to three breaths, then expand again.
- Foggy: open the eyes and include the visual field.
- Over-effort: reduce the size of the field to body plus nearby sounds.
- Constant labeling: use one phrase, “already included,” then stop commentary.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when the user maintains broad awareness for five continuous minutes with no thought chain longer than approximately 30 seconds recognized after the fact, in three of five sessions.

# 8. Q5 — Gap Awareness

**Type:** QCTP synthesis  
**Purpose:** recognize content-light intervals while awareness remains continuous  
**Prerequisite:** Q4 stabilized  
**Typical duration:** 20 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Minute 0:00–5:00 — Regulate and open

Use Q1 for three minutes, then Q4 whole-field awareness for two minutes.

### Minute 5:00–10:00 — Observe thought endings

- Do not try to stop thoughts.
- Notice the moment a thought ends.
- Notice whether another thought begins immediately.
- Rest in any interval without naming it.

### Minute 10:00–15:00 — Reduce intervention

- Stop looking for a gap.
- Let content arise and dissolve.
- When a content-light interval is recognized, do nothing to extend it.

### Minute 15:00–19:00 — Simple awareness

- Release breath control, body scan, and spatial expansion.
- Maintain alertness.
- Use one sparse cue if needed: “Awareness remains.”

### Minute 19:00–20:00 — Return

Feel physical contact, open the eyes, and record whether continuity was maintained.

## State markers

- at least one interval is remembered as content-light rather than unconscious;
- awareness remains present without an active task;
- no demand for imagery or sensation;
- the user can distinguish the interval from a memory gap;
- effort decreases when the interval occurs.

## Look-alikes

- falling asleep;
- blanking from fatigue;
- dissociative detachment;
- staring at darkness with intense effort;
- suppressing thoughts.

## Corrections

- Sleep gaps: sit upright, shorten silence, practice earlier.
- Anxiety in stillness: return to body contact and Q1.
- Trying too hard: return to open monitoring and stop searching.
- Dissociated/unreal feeling persists after return: end the practice, orient physically, and hold progression.

## Progression gate

Accessed when a remembered content-light interval occurs without loss of alertness. Stabilized when intervals recur across three sessions and the user can remain in low-content awareness for at least one continuous minute without forcing.

# 9. TC-PC — Point Consciousness

**Type:** Thomas Campbell source-specific target with an original QCTP induction  
**Public source description:** a stable meditation condition in which ordinary physical sensory input and intellectual noise become less dominant, providing a platform for intuitive or nonphysical exploration  
**Prerequisite:** Q3 stabilized and Q4 accessed  
**Typical duration:** 25 minutes

## Exact QCTP recipe

### Minute 0:00–5:00 — Regulate

Use Q1. At minute five, release all deliberate breath control.

### Minute 5:00–10:00 — Reduce physical engagement

- Hold the body still without strain.
- Include contact sensations, then stop checking each one.
- Let breathing become automatic.
- Do not attempt to numb the body.

### Minute 10:00–15:00 — Quiet the intellectual channel

- Use one simple object, such as the sensation at the nostrils, until thought chains shorten.
- Stop counting.
- When language appears, do not continue the sentence.

### Minute 15:00–20:00 — Dark-field awareness

- Notice the visual field behind closed eyes exactly as it is.
- Do not force blackness or search for lights.
- Let awareness feel simple and centerless or point-like according to direct experience.
- Physical location may become less salient; do not demand that result.

### Minute 20:00–24:00 — Hold nonverbal intent

- Select one simple intent before the session, such as “remain aware” or “be open to useful information.”
- At this phase, recall the intent once without repeating words.
- Remain receptive without asking multiple questions.

### Minute 24:00–25:00 — Return

Feel hands, feet, breathing, room, and date/time. Open the eyes.

## State markers

- body location or sensory demand becomes less prominent;
- awareness remains alert and continuous;
- internal narration is low;
- visual darkness is observed without ocular strain;
- one nonverbal intent can be held without repeated verbal rehearsal;
- the user is not asleep.

## Look-alikes

- forced black visual field;
- dissociation;
- dream imagery absorption;
- ordinary relaxation with continuous thought;
- expectation-driven search for paranormal content.

## Corrections

- Ocular strain: relax the eyes and include sound.
- Thought loops: return to one tactile breath location for one minute.
- Sleepiness: upright posture, eyes slightly open, shorter session.
- Fear: restore body contact and end without functional exploration.
- Imagery flood: label “imagery,” let it pass, return to the simple field.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when the user reports reduced physical salience, low narration, continuous alertness, and stable intent for five minutes in three of five sessions. This gate precedes Campbell-style remote-information or healing experiments.

# 10. M-F10 — Focus 10 Candidate State

**Type:** Monroe source-specific target; original QCTP induction  
**Target:** mind awake, body asleep  
**Prerequisite:** Q1 stabilized, Q3 stabilized, no active safety hold  
**Typical duration:** 35 minutes

## Exact QCTP recipe

### Minute 0:00–5:00 — Entry

- Lie down or use a reclined supported position.
- Use 4/6 breathing at low volume for three minutes.
- Stop counting and allow automatic breathing.

### Minute 5:00–15:00 — Systematic physical release

Move attention once through:

- forehead and eyes;
- jaw and tongue;
- neck and shoulders;
- arms and hands;
- chest and back;
- abdomen and pelvis;
- thighs;
- calves;
- feet.

At each region:

- feel contact and effort;
- reduce effort slightly on the exhale;
- do not return repeatedly to check.

### Minute 15:00–23:00 — Reduce movement and sensory checking

- Decide once that the body is safe and supported.
- Stop adjusting unless pain or numbness creates a physical safety issue.
- Let the body breathe itself.
- Notice heaviness, warmth, numbness, floating, body-size change, or no dramatic sensation.
- Do not chase vibration.

### Minute 23:00–30:00 — Hover at the sleep boundary

- Allow hypnagogic fragments to appear without entering their storyline.
- Maintain one thread of awareness: “I remain aware.”
- The guide provides sparse checks no more often than every two minutes.
- If the instruction is lost, the session has moved into sleep rather than stable Focus 10.

### Minute 30:00–33:00 — State check

Without moving:

- Can the user hear and understand the cue?
- Does breathing feel automatic?
- Is the body heavy, remote, numb, or difficult to locate?
- Is memory continuous?

Do not mentally answer in sentences. Notice the markers.

### Minute 33:00–35:00 — Return

- deepen the breath slightly;
- feel the bed or chair;
- move fingers and toes;
- open eyes;
- sit up slowly.

## State markers

- body movement is very low;
- breathing feels automatic;
- heaviness, numbness, remoteness, floating, or altered body map is present;
- hypnagogic imagery may occur but is not required;
- the user follows and remembers cues;
- alertness remains at least score 2.

## Look-alikes

- ordinary relaxation;
- full sleep;
- sleep paralysis with panic;
- physically tense immobility;
- imagined “body asleep” language without body-state change.

## Corrections

- Ordinary relaxation: extend release phase, reduce adjustments, practice when naturally sleepy but not exhausted.
- Full sleep: earlier time, reclined rather than flat, shorter induction, more frequent sparse checks.
- Panic: deepen breathing, move fingers, open eyes, orient; hold progression.
- Physical tension: allow one deliberate adjustment, then restart the stillness phase.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when at least three of the following occur in three of five sessions for five continuous minutes: automatic breathing, heavy/remote body, altered body map, low movement, hypnagogia with retained awareness, continuous recall. A dramatic vibration is not required.

# 11. M-F12 — Focus 12 Candidate State

**Type:** Monroe source-specific target; original QCTP expansion practice  
**Target:** expanded awareness beyond ordinary body-centered sensory focus  
**Prerequisite:** M-F10 stabilized  
**Typical duration:** 30 minutes

## Exact QCTP recipe

### Minute 0:00–12:00 — Enter Focus 10

Use the abbreviated, already-trained Focus 10 induction. Do not attempt Focus 12 from ordinary baseline.

### Minute 12:00–16:00 — Expand around the body

- Include the space immediately surrounding the body.
- Do not imagine a glowing aura unless the session explicitly trains imagery.
- Notice awareness beyond the skin boundary.

### Minute 16:00–21:00 — Expand to room-volume

- Include the room as one volume.
- Let sounds appear within the field.
- Body sensations remain included but are no longer the center.

### Minute 21:00–26:00 — Nonlocal attentional experiment

- Select one distant or abstract reference, such as the sky above the building or a known location.
- Do not construct detailed imagery.
- Notice whether awareness can remain broad while the reference is held.

### Minute 26:00–28:00 — Optional single inquiry

Only after stable expansion, hold one question nonverbally. Do not demand an answer.

### Minute 28:00–30:00 — Contract and return

- return awareness to room;
- return to body contact;
- move fingers and toes;
- open eyes.

## State markers

- Focus 10 markers remain present;
- body is not the exclusive center of attention;
- awareness feels broader or less localized;
- clarity remains adequate;
- one inquiry can be held without collapsing into analysis.

## Look-alikes

- visualizing a large bubble while remaining fully body-centered;
- diffuse drowsiness;
- dissociation;
- grandiose interpretation of ordinary sensations.

## Corrections

- State collapses: return to Focus 10 rather than forcing expansion.
- Excess imagery: include sound and room-volume.
- Fear or disorientation: contract awareness to body contact and return.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when broad awareness is maintained for five minutes without loss of Focus 10 markers in three sessions.

# 12. QR — Remote-Information Receiver State

**Type:** QCTP experimental synthesis  
**Purpose:** reduce analysis, capture first-pass sensory impressions, and preserve raw data before interpretation  
**Prerequisite:** Q3 stabilized; Q4 or TC-PC accessed  
**Typical duration:** 15–25 minutes plus feedback

## Exact recipe

### Preparation

- The target is selected and concealed before the session.
- The user receives only a random target ID or neutral task statement.
- No descriptive clues are permitted.
- Paper, stylus, or voice capture is ready before state entry.

### Minute 0:00–3:00 — Balance

Use 5 seconds in / 5 seconds out for two minutes, then natural breathing.

### Minute 3:00–5:00 — Receiver state

- open-monitor the body and room;
- reduce narration;
- hold the target ID once;
- release the ID.

### Minute 5:00–10:00 — First-pass data

Record immediately:

- temperature;
- texture;
- color/light;
- sound;
- movement;
- scale;
- density;
- shape;
- spatial arrangement;
- emotional atmosphere.

Use descriptors before nouns.

### Minute 10:00–15:00 — Sketch and relationships

- draw major lines, masses, boundaries, and relative positions;
- record any noun or story in a separate `Analytical Overlay` field;
- return to descriptors after recording the overlay.

### End

- stop before searching for certainty;
- lock the raw record;
- view feedback only after the record is saved;
- score correspondence separately from confidence.

## State markers

- first impressions are brief and recorded before explanation;
- descriptors outnumber nouns;
- analytical stories are separated;
- target feedback is unavailable during capture;
- the user can stop rather than rescue a weak session.

## Look-alikes

- guessing from target number;
- constructing a scene;
- editing impressions to make them coherent;
- judging accuracy before feedback;
- retrofitting the record after feedback.

## Progression gate

Functional when the user completes ten blinded sessions with preserved raw data and feedback scoring. Progress is based on calibration and correspondence, not one striking session.

# 13. QO — OBE Threshold and Separation Practice

**Type:** QCTP original practice informed by Monroe/Buhlman/Campbell source traditions  
**Purpose:** maintain awareness as body processing recedes and test nonphysical-movement imagery without physical movement  
**Prerequisite:** M-F10 stabilized  
**Typical duration:** 35–45 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Phase 1 — Enter Focus 10

Use the trained Focus 10 induction. Do not begin separation imagery from ordinary relaxation.

### Phase 2 — Stabilize for five minutes

- breathing automatic;
- body still;
- awareness continuous;
- no separation attempt yet.

### Phase 3 — Kinesthetic movement tests

Perform each for 30–60 seconds while physical muscles remain still:

1. imagine the right hand lifting from inside the felt hand;
2. imagine the whole body rocking a few degrees left and right;
3. imagine floating upward six inches;
4. imagine rolling to one side without moving the physical body.

Stop any method that causes actual muscle contraction.

### Phase 4 — Select one separation method

Use only the method with the strongest nonphysical movement sensation. Do not cycle rapidly through techniques.

### Phase 5 — Attempt

- maintain calm;
- increase the imagined movement gradually;
- do not hold the breath;
- do not wait for vibrations as a mandatory signal;
- if a clear shift occurs, move attention away from the physical body and toward a simple destination within the room.

### Phase 6 — Return

- state the intention to return;
- feel physical breathing;
- move fingers and toes;
- open eyes;
- orient fully before standing.

## State markers

- stable Focus 10 prerequisite;
- nonphysical movement sensation stronger than deliberate visualization alone;
- physical muscles remain still;
- fear remains manageable;
- continuity and recall remain intact.

## Look-alikes

- physically rocking;
- breath holding;
- chasing vibrations;
- falling asleep and dreaming of movement;
- sleep paralysis interpreted as automatic success.

## Corrections

- Physical movement: reduce imagined amplitude.
- Fear: return to body contact; no forced continuation.
- No movement sensation: return to Focus 10 practice for another session.
- Sleep paralysis: slow exhale, move one finger or toe, orient; do not escalate the practice while panicked.

## Progression gate

Functional OBE credit requires a coherent episode record with continuity, entry method, environment description, return, and later verification where possible. Threshold sensations alone earn state-training credit, not OBE verification.

# 14. QI — Stable Imaginality Environment

**Type:** QCTP original Studio/Lab practice informed by Campbell’s public Tom’s Park concept and creative-reception methods  
**Purpose:** build a stable repeatable inner environment, train sensory detail, and distinguish deliberate construction from spontaneous development  
**Prerequisite:** Q3 stabilized and Q4 accessed  
**Typical duration:** 20–30 minutes

## Exact recipe

### Minute 0:00–5:00 — Enter

Use Q1 and Q3. Release deliberate breath pacing.

### Minute 5:00–10:00 — Create one anchor location

Choose one simple location:

- a room;
- a workshop;
- a clearing;
- a platform;
- a geometric chamber.

Define only:

- floor/ground;
- one boundary;
- one light source;
- one central object.

### Minute 10:00–15:00 — Add senses one at a time

- visual form;
- touch/contact;
- sound;
- temperature;
- movement.

Do not add story characters yet.

### Minute 15:00–20:00 — Stability test

- turn attention away from the central object;
- return to it;
- verify whether size, location, and relation remain stable;
- intentionally change one property and observe whether the change holds.

### Minute 20:00–25:00 — Receptive interval

- stop adding content;
- ask one open question;
- record any spontaneous image, phrase, movement, or change after the session;
- label each event as `deliberately constructed`, `spontaneous candidate`, or `uncertain`.

### Minute 25:00–30:00 — Exit and map

Open the eyes and sketch the environment from memory. Record differences from prior visits.

## State markers

- anchor location can be revisited;
- basic geometry remains stable;
- at least two sensory channels are present;
- deliberate changes can be distinguished from spontaneous candidates;
- the user exits on command and remembers the sequence.

## Look-alikes

- uncontrolled fantasy story;
- trying to make imagery photorealistic;
- interpreting every spontaneous change as external information;
- falling asleep into a dream.

## Corrections

- Unstable scene: reduce to one object and one boundary.
- No visual imagery: emphasize touch, sound, and spatial relationship.
- Story takes over: return to the anchor object and stop adding characters.
- Drowsiness: upright posture and shorter session.

## Progression gate

Stabilized when the same environment can be re-entered and mapped with consistent anchor relationships across three sessions. Functional when it can support one problem-solving or receptive task without becoming an uncontrolled narrative.

# 15. State-session record contract

Every session record must contain:

- state ID;
- guidance tier;
- start and end time;
- posture;
- breath method;
- state-marker scores;
- alertness;
- effort;
- fear/anxiety;
- memory continuity;
- primary failure mode;
- correction used;
- functional task attempted;
- raw observations;
- interpretation;
- outcome/feedback when available;
- capability level before and after;
- next permitted session.

# 16. State Atlas acceptance criteria

The atlas is implemented when:

1. each state appears as a clear app card;
2. source-specific labels remain intact;
3. prerequisites and gates are enforced;
4. exact recipes are available in text and timed audio;
5. state markers are session-specific;
6. look-alikes and correction rules are visible before or after practice without cluttering the active meditation;
7. voice-first debrief is available immediately;
8. the app can recommend remediation rather than blindly advance;
9. reduced-guidance tests exist;
10. physical-iPhone sessions verify timing, audio, pause/resume, offline assets, and data persistence.
