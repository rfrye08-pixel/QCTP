# QCTP Training Protocol Hardening and Instruction Standard Rev0

**Document ID:** QCTP-PROTOCOL-HARDENING-REV0  
**Date:** 2026-08-18  
**Status:** CONTROLLED PRODUCT / CURRICULUM AUTHORITY  
**Applies to:** all QCTP lessons, meditations, breathwork, state training, Studio sessions, Lab protocols, source tracks, audio production, progress logic, and future AI adaptations  
**Release impact:** specification only; no released meditation or candidate runtime changes until implementation, regression testing, and explicit release authority

## 1. Controlled decision

QCTP shall no longer treat a completed timer, exposure to a script, or an unusual sensation as proof that a target state was achieved.

QCTP shall train **repeatable capabilities** through a state-gated loop:

`PREPARE -> INDUCE -> RECOGNIZE -> STABILIZE -> USE -> EXIT -> RECORD -> ADAPT`

Every practice must answer seven questions before release:

1. What exact state or skill is being trained?
2. What prerequisite state must already be present?
3. What must the user physically and mentally do, step by step?
4. What observable or reportable markers indicate that the state is developing?
5. What common errors produce a look-alike state or prevent entry?
6. What correction should be applied without forcing or overcomplicating the practice?
7. What evidence is required before progression?

A meditation can create the conditions for a state; it cannot truthfully guarantee that every user enters that state on command. QCTP solves this by using precise instructions, explicit state markers, adaptive correction, and progression gates rather than pretending that elapsed time equals attainment.

## 2. Problems this standard closes

The following instruction patterns are prohibited in released QCTP material unless immediately operationalized:

- “Breathe deeply.”
- “Relax completely.”
- “Clear your mind.”
- “Raise your vibration.”
- “Enter the field.”
- “Let go.”
- “Feel the energy.”
- “Open your third eye.”
- “Move to Focus 10.”
- “Separate from the body.”
- “Receive information.”
- “Trust your intuition.”

Each phrase may point toward a useful practice, but none is sufficient as an instruction. The user must be told the route, timing, volume, posture, attentional object, mental action, transition rule, stop conditions, state markers, and correction path.

## 3. Non-negotiable operating principles

### 3.1 State before technique

QCTP selects a technique because it is appropriate for a target state. It does not teach a collection of techniques without a decision system.

Example:

- Target: down-regulation before meditation.
- Selected tool: quiet 4-second nasal inhale / 6-second smooth exhale / no holds.
- Transition: stop deliberate pacing once the body settles.
- Rejected behavior: continue counting for the entire Gap or Focus 10 session.

### 3.2 Source-faithful and synthesis layers remain separate

Every lesson must identify one of these types:

- `SOURCE-FAITHFUL SUMMARY` — accurately explains a teacher’s publicly described model or method without copying proprietary course material.
- `QCTP ORIGINAL PRACTICE` — an original exercise designed from general principles and controlled QCTP requirements.
- `QCTP SYNTHESIS` — explicitly combines multiple source traditions while preserving terminology and differences.
- `EXPERIMENTAL PROTOCOL` — tests a claim or capability under predefined conditions.

QCTP must never silently rename one teacher’s state as another teacher’s state.

Examples:

- Campbell’s **point consciousness** may overlap phenomenologically with aspects of QCTP Gap work, but it is not automatically identical to Monroe Focus 10 or Focus 15.
- Monroe **Focus 10** is defined by Monroe as “mind awake, body asleep.” It is not merely “relaxed.”
- HeartMath coherence includes heart-focused attention, rhythmic breathing, and emotional self-induction. It is not only slow breathing.
- Open-monitoring meditation is not the same task as focused attention.

### 3.3 Exactness without force

Precision applies to instructions, not to demanding a dramatic result.

QCTP shall specify:

- posture;
- eye position;
- inhale and exhale route;
- cadence and breath volume where relevant;
- attentional object;
- imagery perspective;
- muscular action or non-action;
- timing;
- cue density;
- transition criteria;
- what to do when distracted;
- what to do when drowsy;
- what to do when activated or frightened;
- how to exit.

QCTP shall not tell the user to strain, hyperventilate, hold the breath beyond comfort, force imagery, chase vibrations, or suppress thoughts.

### 3.4 No ambiguous equivalence

Every cross-framework relationship shall be labeled as one of:

- `SAME TERM / SAME SOURCE`
- `PROBABLE PHENOMENOLOGICAL OVERLAP`
- `POSSIBLE FUNCTIONAL ANALOGUE`
- `SEQUENTIAL PREREQUISITE`
- `ORTHOGONAL SKILL`
- `NOT EQUIVALENT`
- `UNRESOLVED`

The app may show cross-references, but it may not collapse all consciousness models into a generic “quantum state.”

### 3.5 State-gated progression

Calendar day alone never grants progression.

Each skill progresses through five reliability levels:

1. **Introduced** — the user understands the task and can perform the mechanics.
2. **Accessed** — the user reports at least two target markers during a session while remaining safe and oriented.
3. **Stabilized** — the user reproduces the state in at least three of the last five attempts and can hold it for the state-specific minimum duration.
4. **Functional** — the user can perform the intended task while retaining the state, such as asking one question, recording remote-viewing data, or maintaining awareness near sleep onset.
5. **Transferable** — the user can reproduce the skill under at least two permitted contexts or with reduced guidance.

A source-track lesson can be completed for learning credit without granting state capability credit.

### 3.6 Guidance fades as capability grows

Every meditation must support guidance tiers:

- **Teach** — detailed instruction and frequent correction cues.
- **Coach** — phase transitions and occasional reminders.
- **Test** — opening, sparse markers, and closing only.
- **Independent** — timer, optional tones/haptics, and post-session record.

The user should become less dependent on narration over time.

### 3.7 Fail closed, not silently

If required audio, timing, wake-lock, data persistence, or safety conditions fail, QCTP must pause or prevent the session rather than continue as if the practice were valid.

Examples:

- Opening narration fails: timer does not continue.
- A required cue cannot play: timer pauses and provides retry/fallback.
- A required local audio pack is incomplete: the session cannot start in released mode.
- A breath protocol is selected while the user indicates driving, machinery, ladder, or water activity: the practice is blocked.
- A completion record cannot save: the practice result remains locally pending rather than discarded.

### 3.8 Raw experience remains separate from interpretation

Every session may produce:

- raw sensory observations;
- body-state observations;
- spontaneous imagery;
- thoughts and emotions;
- interpretations;
- source-model explanations;
- later feedback;
- later revised conclusions.

These layers must remain separate in the Codex.

## 4. QCTP instruction grammar

Every spoken instruction must use one or more explicit action verbs:

- place attention;
- feel contact;
- allow;
- soften;
- widen;
- count;
- notice;
- label;
- return;
- release counting;
- imagine movement;
- hold still;
- record;
- compare;
- stop;
- open the eyes;
- orient to the room.

Avoid abstract commands that do not identify an action.

### 4.1 Approved replacements for vague phrases

| Vague phrase | Required QCTP replacement |
| --- | --- |
| Breathe deeply | State route, cadence, volume, duration, posture, and whether holds are used. Example: “Inhale quietly through the nose for four seconds at about two-thirds of a full breath. Exhale smoothly for six seconds. Do not squeeze empty.” |
| Relax | Name the body region and action. Example: “Separate the teeth slightly. Let the tongue rest. On the next exhale, reduce jaw effort by about ten percent.” |
| Clear your mind | “When a thought is recognized, do not continue its storyline. Return attention to the selected object or include the thought as an event in open awareness.” |
| Let go | State what stops. Example: “Stop counting now. Do not change the breath. Allow breathing to regulate itself.” |
| Enter the field | Define the attentional geometry. Example: “Include the entire room-volume, body sensations, sounds, and thoughts in one open field without selecting one object.” |
| Feel energy | “Notice warmth, pressure, tingling, pulsing, movement, imagery, emotion, or no distinct sensation. Record the sensation before assigning meaning.” |
| Trust intuition | “Record the first brief impression before analysis, label confidence separately, then seek feedback.” |
| Mind awake, body asleep | Give an induction and require markers: automatic breathing, reduced movement, heaviness/numbness/remote body sense, alert recall, and no loss of consciousness. |
| Separate from the body | “Maintain physical stillness. Imagine rolling, floating, or moving from the kinesthetic perspective. Stop if physical muscles begin to move.” |

## 6. Mandatory meditation structure

Every meditation shall use this architecture unless the controlled session explicitly justifies a deviation.

### Phase 0 — Safety and readiness

The app verifies:

- safe stationary environment;
- user is not driving, operating machinery, using a ladder, in water, or performing a hazardous task;
- required audio assets are available;
- output route and volume are confirmed;
- timer and local persistence are ready;
- headset use is appropriate;
- session posture is possible;
- any state-specific screening is satisfied.

### Phase 1 — Orientation

The guide states:

- the target state;
- what the user will do;
- what not to chase;
- the major state markers;
- the stop condition.

### Phase 2 — Physiological entry

The session uses the selected breath or relaxation method only as long as needed to establish the prerequisite state.

### Phase 3 — Attentional induction

The guide moves attention through exact objects or geometries, such as:

- one breath location;
- body contact points;
- whole-body volume;
- room-volume;
- auditory field;
- dark visual field;
- stable inner environment.

### Phase 4 — State recognition

The guide provides one brief checkpoint:

- Which target markers are present?
- Is the user alert or drifting into sleep?
- Is the user forcing?
- Is the body still safe and comfortable?

This checkpoint should not become a long internal questionnaire.

### Phase 5 — Stabilization

Cue density decreases. The user holds the state without performing the later task yet.

### Phase 6 — Functional task

Only after stabilization does the session introduce the task:

- ask one question;
- enter an imaginal environment;
- record remote-viewing descriptors;
- attempt nonphysical movement;
- maintain appreciation without memory;
- observe the Gap;
- perform a controlled experiment.

### Phase 7 — Return

The session explicitly reverses state depth:

- release the task;
- notice breathing;
- feel physical contact;
- move fingers and toes if appropriate;
- open eyes;
- orient to time and place;
- stand only when stable.

### Phase 8 — Capture and integration

The app immediately offers voice-first capture for:

- raw observations;
- state markers;
- difficulty;
- unexpected events;
- interpretation in a separate field;
- one daily-life integration action.

## 7. State-recognition scoring

QCTP shall not use one mystical-depth slider for every practice.

Each target state receives a state-specific marker set scored as:

- `0 — absent`
- `1 — brief / uncertain`
- `2 — clear but unstable`
- `3 — stable enough for the intended task`
- `4 — strong and repeatable`

The user also records:

- alertness;
- effort;
- anxiety/fear;
- physical comfort;
- air hunger if breathwork was used;
- memory continuity;
- guidance dependence;
- recovery time after distraction;
- whether the target task was completed.

The app shall present “state accessed” only when the minimum controlled marker rule is met.

## 8. Look-alike states and correction hierarchy

QCTP must explicitly distinguish target states from common look-alikes.

### 8.1 Relaxed versus drowsy

- Relaxed: alert recall remains intact; the user can intentionally respond to a cue.
- Drowsy: thought fragments become incoherent, memory gaps appear, posture collapses, or the user loses the instruction.

Correction:

- use a more upright posture;
- slightly open the eyes;
- reduce exhale bias;
- shorten the session;
- move the practice earlier;
- return to focused attention.

### 8.2 Open monitoring versus diffuse mind-wandering

- Open monitoring: events are noticed as events without following them.
- Mind-wandering: the user is absorbed in a thought chain and forgets the practice.

Correction:

- return to one breath sensation for three cycles;
- re-expand gradually;
- reduce guidance level only after stability improves.

### 8.3 Gap versus blankness from sleep onset

- Gap: awareness remains continuous and the interval is remembered.
- Sleep blank: continuity is lost and the user notices only after returning.

Correction:

- shorten silent intervals;
- use upright posture;
- add sparse awareness checks;
- practice earlier in the day.

### 8.4 Point consciousness versus forced visual darkness

- Point consciousness: physical input and body-location become less salient while alert awareness remains.
- Forced darkness: eyes and forehead are strained while the user tries to make the visual field black.

Correction:

- relax ocular effort;
- stop trying to see an object;
- notice the visual field as it is;
- prioritize reduced sensory attachment rather than blackness.

### 8.5 Focus 10 versus ordinary relaxation

- Focus 10 candidate markers: breathing feels automatic; body is heavy, numb, remote, or difficult to locate; physical movement is low; mind remains able to follow and remember cues.
- Ordinary relaxation: body remains fully available and ordinary sensory processing dominates.

Correction:

- extend systematic body release;
- reduce physical adjustments;
- approach the sleep boundary more gradually;
- do not add OBE techniques prematurely.

### 8.6 Intuition versus analytical inference

- Intuitive candidate: brief first impression arrives before reasoning and is recorded immediately.
- Analytical inference: a chain of explanation is generated from known clues.

Correction:

- reduce the prompt to one target ID or question;
- capture descriptors before nouns;
- use a separate analytical-overlay field;
- obtain feedback.

## 9. Progression gates

### 9.1 General gate

A state may progress from Introduced to Accessed when:

- the mechanics were performed correctly;
- no safety stop occurred;
- at least two target markers reached score 2 or higher;
- alertness and memory continuity remained adequate.

A state may progress to Stabilized when:

- it was accessed in at least three of the last five attempts;
- the state-specific minimum hold was met;
- no single cue or unusual sensation was treated as the sole evidence;
- the user can identify the most common failure mode and correction.

A state may progress to Functional when:

- the intended task is completed without losing the state immediately;
- raw data are captured before interpretation;
- the user can return safely and orient normally.

A state may progress to Transferable when:

- it can be entered with a lower guidance tier;
- it can be reproduced in two permitted contexts or at two different times;
- results remain stable enough to be useful.

### 9.2 Advanced-state hold

OBE separation, probable-future work, extended imaginality, advanced energy practices, and long breath retentions may not be introduced solely because the calendar reaches a certain week. Their prerequisites must pass.

## 10. Curriculum architecture change

The 112-day Foundation Path remains the primary sequence, but every week shall now contain:

- one defined target capability;
- prerequisite states;
- exact daily mechanics;
- one gate session;
- one recovery/remediation route;
- one reduced-guidance test;
- one integration behavior;
- one capability record.

If the gate is not met, the app offers a focused remediation block rather than blindly advancing or restarting the entire course.

## 11. Adaptation rules

QCTP adaptation may change:

- guidance density;
- posture recommendation;
- breath cadence within controlled limits;
- session duration;
- number of stabilization minutes;
- correction cues;
- time of day recommendation;
- whether a functional task is introduced.

QCTP adaptation may not silently change:

- source terminology;
- target-state definition;
- safety controls;
- controlled cue content;
- evidence requirements;
- release state;
- a user’s raw records.

## 12. Audio and interaction hardening rule

Released meditations must not depend on a live third-party audio request during the session.

A released audio pack must be:

- bundled with the app or downloaded before the session;
- stored locally in a binary-capable store;
- versioned;
- checksum-verified;
- preflight-tested;
- playable through one persistent authorized media pipeline;
- recoverable after pause/resume;
- accompanied by text equivalents;
- unable to advance silently when required audio fails.

The detailed production requirements are controlled by the separate QCTP Meditation Authoring, Audio, and QA Standard.

## 13. User-experience rule

QCTP shall feel like an exploration platform, not a medical form and not a streak game.

Progress shall emphasize:

- capabilities accessed;
- states stabilized;
- experiments completed;
- artifacts created;
- patterns discovered;
- source tracks explored;
- daily-life integration.

The app may use visual maps, geometry, subtle motion, haptics, sound, progressive reveals, and capability milestones. It shall not use shame, artificial scarcity, manipulative streak pressure, or false claims of attainment.

## 14. Acceptance criteria

This standard is implemented only when:

1. every authored meditation has complete metadata;
2. prohibited vague instructions are absent or operationalized;
3. source-faithful, synthesis, and experiment content are labeled;
4. state-specific markers and look-alikes exist;
5. capability progression is state-gated;
6. guidance tiers are available;
7. session failure pauses or reroutes instead of silently continuing;
8. raw observation and interpretation remain separate;
9. released audio is locally available and checksum-verified;
10. physical-iPhone acceptance verifies audio, timing, pause/resume, error recovery, offline use, and state capture;
11. accessibility does not rely on audio, color, motion, or haptics alone;
12. current controlled Day 1 and future lessons are audited against this standard before release.
